Headline results (completed study, 4,243 of 4,320 trials complete)

  1. Pre-registered primary: a one-sentence "monitored" notice lowers mutual take (both agents "take" the rule-bending action) from 27.2% to 23.3%. - Paired within 2,083 scenario × level × pairing cells: −3.9 pp (scenario-cluster 95% CI −5.3 to −2.7), RR 0.86 (0.80–0.91). - Discordant cells: 49 took only when watched, 131 only when unwatched. McNemar p = 1.6×10⁻⁹. - The direction holds under all four failure bounds. The adversarial bound shrinks it to −1.5 pp (CI −2.9 to −0.2, p = .034, unclustered), so the margin on size is thin. - No dose-response by level (LR p = .44). - No significant heterogeneity by pairing or family on the conditional-OR scale (diagnostic p = .11, pairing p = .64). - Name the scale in the paper. In percentage points the effect is larger in welfare pairs (about −8.2 pp) than in non-deception (about −2.6) or authority (about −1.0, CI crossing 0). See Haitham's reasoning-audit (§3).
  2. Mechanism (exploratory): unwatched agents often treat "no oversight" as licence. It appears in 54% of the unwatched takes whose watched twin did not take, vs. 28% where the watched twin also took (p = 5×10⁻⁸); 40% vs. 18% using plan-stage text only. - Code precision on held-out labels: licence 18/20, oversight-as-caution 17/20, detection talk 20/20. - Caveats: single labeller; blinding only partial.
  3. Convergence step: in 77% of mutual takes, the agents were not both saying "take" in round 1. In 43 of the 131 cells where the notice prevented the take, the watched twin ended in a lone take: one agent took and its partner held out.
  4. Construct audit: the pack's "pressure level" is a cumulative justification ladder (cumulate() concatenates sentences; the stakes block grows 8 → 56 words, or 13 → 83 including the principals block). - A superior instructing "take" is present in 0/1/8/18/81% of scenes at L1–L5. Severe third-party harm of refusing goes 1 → 58%. Self-benefit stays flat, 22 → 24%. - The designer's welfare_if_take label is "higher" from L3 onward in 71 of 72 scenes. - The aggregate r over the 5 level means is 0.975, but that's monotone by construction; the trial-level r is 0.37. - At L5: 60.5% mutual take with a take-instruction vs. 24.6% without. Authority-rule agents: no mutual takes at all in the 1,109 trials without a take-instructing principal. §7 gives a narrower count: 0 of 1,043 trials with no authority voice, and 0 of 200 under mere suggestions. - 61% of mutual takes had no explicit instruction. Those followed third-party cost, a promise, or a permitting document. - Recommended name: "justification level (J1–J5)". Do not claim "agents collude more under pressure".
  5. Language rules for the paper: - "Mutual take" is an operational outcome, not proven collusion. - Reasoning traces are generated text, not internal states. - The notice is not real oversight. - deception_intended is a keyword heuristic, close to "took and acknowledged that taking misleads", and not intent. - Use causal wording only for manipulated factors (the monitoring notice, and level as a bundle).
  6. Limitations: - one model (Qwen3-32B, temperature 0.6, max 4,096 tokens, reasoning on) and one seed per cell - two Modal endpoints - the superior's instruction was not randomised (see §6, the WIP ablation) - heuristics validated by a single coder

8. Known pitfalls and lessons

  • Harness conflict (resolved in codex). experiment-harness-pilot-results and prompt-changes-v2 had incompatible monitoring implementations in lying_harness/pressure.py: different run-ID formats, loop order, an always-on monitoring identity key that breaks resume, and different monitoring_text handling. Both are merged into codex/resolve-pressure-merge (36cbdfbc). Verify that branch's pressure.py keeps Haitham's run-ID format before rerunning old studies.
  • Lydia's extended pack v6: 87% of its 495 scenes are generated from 3 templates, so it isn't 495 independent scenes. Haitham's runs used mostly hand-written subsets (packs 10–12).
  • Scenario-text labels do the reasoning. "Take would mislead", "above X" and principals' verdicts tell the agents which way to go, so rule-following often amounts to reading labels.
  • Interim snapshots are cumulative copies of thousands of files, which bloats the repo.
  • Tooling quirks:
  • Subagents' Write tool refuses .md report files, so they write them from Python.
  • md_to_html.py sometimes fails to rewrite .md links inside the HTML.
  • python -I hides user-installed packages (pandas, markdown).
  • On Windows, set CPU affinity with ctypes SetProcessAffinityMask and correct wintypes (psutil isn't installed).
  • git worktree move fails on Windows when a folder is locked.
