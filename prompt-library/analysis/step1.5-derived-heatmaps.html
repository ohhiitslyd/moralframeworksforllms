Step 1.5: Derived-metric heat maps

Use it for: a fast visual of every derived or engineered outcome from Step 1: interaction metrics, vocabulary, plans vs. actions, and deception flags, broken down by every design factor. It runs straight after Step 1, before any statistics. Pipeline stage: after Step 1, before Step 2. Like Step 0.5, it can feed a Step 3 draft early. Origin: owner request (2026-10-25): "add step 1.5 for any derived / engineered feature output variables." The reference implementation is tools/step1.5_derived_heatmaps.py. On a 3,026-episode table it produced 71 figures in about 90 seconds. Previous: Step 0.5 (raw outcomes) and Step 1.

Reference script: tools/step1.5_derived_heatmaps.py is specific to the 2026-10-25 pressure study's Step 1 table. Pass --table, --schema and --out explicitly, because its defaults assume that run's folder layout. Adapt FACTORS / ALIASES for other designs.

Prompt

Make heat maps of the derived metrics in <TABLE>, using <SCHEMA>, and write them to <OUTPUT_DIR>.

Inputs (fill in) - <TABLE>: Step 1's trial_metrics.csv - <SCHEMA>: Step 1's metrics_schema.json - <OUTPUT_DIR> - <FACTORS>: default is level, monitoring, pairing, diagnostic or family, setting, and, for agent-level metrics, the agent's own criterion

Rules - Pick metrics from the schema: role outcome, in the agent or pair sections, of type binary, numeric or ordinal. Skip empty and constant columns. - Agent-level metrics: pool each _a / _b pair into one metric over agents, and use the agent's own criterion as a factor. - Binary metrics: show rates on an absolute 0–100% scale. - Numeric metrics: show medians, shaded within their column, with the value printed. Say clearly that the shading is per column. - Mark validation status in the labels: - ⚠ unreliable: heuristics whose schema validation says reliable: false - (unvalidated): heuristics with no validation - Monitoring contrasts: when a heuristic's accuracy differs by experimental arm, state it in the README, and don't read anything into its watched vs. unwatched difference. - Column names: tolerate naming differences between table versions, e.g. diagnostic vs. scenario_diagnostic. Skip a factor that's absent, rather than failing. - Every cell prints its value and n. Write a CSV behind every chart. Use plain-word labels. - Local compute: run under the reserved-CPU launcher (python tools/run_reserved.py <script>). Never kill processes by name. - Interim snapshots: label every figure PRELIMINARY.

Deliverables 1. One factor × derived metrics: pair-level and agent-level separately, with rates and medians separately. Split into chunks of about 10 metrics per figure. 2. Two factors × derived metric: for the binary metrics that vary most across the main factor (e.g. pressure level), heat maps of level × monitoring, pairing × monitoring, family × monitoring, and level × pairing. 3. README.md (+ .html): the source table, the metric counts, the reliability legend, how to regenerate, every figure linked, and the highlights embedded. 4. The script, so it can be rerun.

Report back: the figure count, the runtime, how many metrics were included and excluded, and 3–5 patterns that stand out. Label those patterns descriptive, and caveat any that involve ⚠ columns.

Don't: run statistical tests or headline ⚠ columns here. Step 2 does the inference.
